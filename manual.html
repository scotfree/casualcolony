§1 Overview

What is this?

Casual Colony is a tap-driven puzzle about a signal network that, this version, has a small colony living inside it. There is no clock — every tap is a turn, and turns are the only kind of time the game keeps. You light connected crystals by spending a limited energy budget, keep a population of colonists fed on what your farms grow, and let mines turn a well-fed colony into more energy to spend. A run ends when the energy runs out, or when nothing left on the board would do anything if you tapped it — whichever comes first.

§2 Quick Start

Playing "Recolonized" — your first ten taps

Recolonized is the level built to walk you through every new system at once. It starts you with 8 energy and an unlit board split into two halves: a small colony up top, and a crystal ring waiting below. Tap the numbered tiles below in order — badges 1–10 — and you'll see feeding, mining income, and a full cascade in your first ten taps.

There's a fourth residential tile on the board — the one marked ? — that was never part of the sequence above. Tap it once: population rises to 4 against only 3 food, and the colony starts starving. The HUD's population readout turns red and energy begins draining. Tap that same tile a second time — an already-active residential tile deactivates instead of lighting again — and the colony is fed again immediately, for free.

You'll likely still have energy left after all ten taps — that's normal for a colony earning more than it spends. See exactly how a run like that actually ends in §3, How a Run Ends.

§3 Mechanics

How everything works

The board and your energy

Every level is a grid of tiles and a fixed energy budget. A tap that actually activates something spends exactly 1 energy, no matter how many tiles it lights — you're paying for the decision, not the payoff. Tapping desert, an already-lit tile, or anything that wouldn't change the board is always free. There's no timer and nothing moves on its own between taps; the board is exactly as patient as you are.

Cascades: signal, activation cost, and boost

A tap hands the tapped tile 1 unit of signal. Every other building type has its own activation cost — how much signal it takes to switch it on — and each tile that activates hands whatever signal is left, minus the next tile's cost, onward to its neighbors. Crystal is cheap (cost 1), so a plain crystal has just enough signal to light itself and nothing more. A power plant is different: it adds a boost (5, by default) to whatever signal passes through it, which is what lets one tap ripple across a whole cluster — and far enough to spill into pricier tiles beyond it.

A lone crystal, tapped directly: signal 1, minus its own cost of 1, plus no boost — 1 − 1 + 0 = 0 — it lights itself and nothing further.

Red and green crystal behave exactly like plain crystal, but only spread along one axis each — red north/south, green east/west — so a level's crystal color isn't cosmetic, it's a constraint on which direction a cluster can grow.

Drain — the tile that costs you progress

A drain tile never lights up itself. It deactivates every currently-lit tile orthogonally next to it, and it fires two ways: tapped directly (costs energy, only if there's something to clear), or reactively, the instant any cascade lights a tile next to it — for free, the same tick. A tile beside a drain effectively can't stay lit.

The colony economy

Residential, farm, and mine tiles activate the same way crystals do — a strong enough crystal chain can spill into one of them — but they're pricier to switch on: cost 2 for residential and farm, cost 3 for a mine, against a crystal's cost of 1. A mine in particular acts as a signal sink: reachable from a well-boosted network, but it eats most of whatever signal is left, so propagation rarely survives past one. Tapping one directly still always works, the same as any tile — a tap is never blocked by cost, only what a cascade has left over to spend beyond the tile you actually tapped. Once active, they feed a second system, recomputed fresh from the board after every tap:

Mines only pay out while the colony is fed — a starving colony's mines sit idle, and the shortfall bleeds energy instead. That drain is what you're avoiding by managing population yourself, tile by tile.

Free culling

Residential is the one building you can turn back off: tap an already-active residential tile and it deactivates, dropping population by one. It's the only free action in the game — no energy cost, and it works even at 0 energy — because it's the player's own tool for fixing a starving colony, rather than the game quietly picking who starves. A paid or blocked cull could leave a starving colony with no way back; a free one never can.

How a run ends

A run resolves the moment nothing about the board can change further — whichever happens first:

  • Energy hits 0. The original rule: no taps left to spend.
  • The board is exhausted. New with mining: if your colony earns more than it spends, energy can climb instead of drain. Once every remaining tile would be a no-op if tapped, the run ends right there — even with energy still on the clock.

Either way, the fraction of the whole board that ended up activated is compared against the level's completion goal: meet or beat it and it's a win, fall short and it's a loss.

§4 Open Questions

What's undecided, and what's not built yet

Casual Colony is a small game that grows one system at a time. Some of what's below is genuinely undecided; the rest is scoped out on purpose, for now.

Still open

Should winning ever be spatial, not just a percentage?
Right now the win condition only cares about the fraction of the whole board activated — not where those tiles are. Contiguous territory, or holding a shape, is a different kind of puzzle that hasn't been tried.
Should placing buildings ever cost energy mid-run?
Right now you can only change what a tile is in the separate level editor, outside a real run, for free. Spending energy to place a building as part of a run — instead of just activating what's already there — is a different game and hasn't been built.
Where should more job types plug in?
Mining was the first colony "job." Sensor towers (revealing fog of war) and defense against dangers were both part of the original pitch — the colony economy was built generally enough for a new job to opt in the same way mining did, as one flag on a building, but neither exists yet.
